# Proposal: Japanese quizzes (JLPT N5 to N3)

**Status: proposal, not in v1 scope.** Nothing here changes a v1 format. The only v1 fact it relies on is that the interface language (`ui_lang`) is separate from the language the quiz is written in (`lang`) ([TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md](../TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md), section 9).

## The idea

Practice tests in the shape of the JLPT: Language Knowledge (Vocabulary), Language Knowledge (Grammar) and Reading, at N5, N4 and N3, with the `jlpt-*` templates below as starting points. Listening comes almost for free, because v1 already embeds audio (`![Track 1](track1.mp3)`) in listening questions.

Every JLPT section is single-answer multiple choice, so the existing `single` question type is enough and answer matching needs nothing new. A JLPT quiz is written in Japanese with an English or Vietnamese interface (`lang: ja`, `ui_lang: en` or `vi`). No Japanese catalog is needed; v1 accepts only `en` and `vi` for `lang`, so `ja` is the addition.

## What works with v1 as it is

- **Japanese text** in questions, choices and explanations. The text is Unicode and passes through unchanged.
- **Fonts.** The embedded fonts (Be Vietnam Pro, Source Serif 4, Lexend) have no kanji, so the browser falls back to the system font on Windows, macOS, Android and iOS. fullmark embeds no Japanese font: a subset of a CJK font adds megabytes to a page that is meant to be one small file. The cost is a look that varies by device, and the contrast and glyph-coverage tests cannot cover Japanese.
- **Vocabulary and Grammar** as a sentence with a blank (`＿＿＿`) and four choices.
- **Listening** with embedded audio and the usual timer.

## What it needs

1. **Shared passages.** Reading questions point at one text, and the format has `pick` banks but nothing that attaches a passage (or a figure, or a notice) to a group of questions. A group would hold the passage once, show it beside its questions, and keep the questions in one section. It also serves IELTS Reading, so it is the most useful part of this proposal and could ship alone.
2. **Furigana.** Reading aids over kanji are the point of N5 to N3 material. Raw HTML is refused (`markdown.raw-html`), so `<ruby>` is not available. A syntax such as `漢字{かんじ}` renders ruby markup, with a check rule for a mismatched or empty reading. The page shows or hides the readings with a switch, so the same quiz serves a learner who wants them and one who is testing without them.
3. **Underlined words.** JLPT asks about "the underlined word", and Markdown has no underline. Bold is the fallback; a real underline needs a small inline syntax.
4. **`lang="ja"` on Japanese text.** Without it a browser may draw Chinese variants of the same kanji. `lang: ja` sets it on the question text; an inline span covers mixed text. The page chrome keeps `<html lang>` from `ui_lang`.
5. **A glyph check.** `check` warns when a quiz uses `font: sans | serif | readable` with Japanese text, since those fonts do not cover it and the page will use whatever the device has.

## Templates

`jlpt-vocabulary` and `jlpt-grammar` (single-answer, four choices, one section per exam part, the exam's timing in the section times), `jlpt-reading` once passages exist. Each at one level, with sample text in Japanese and instructions in English. They are ordinary built-in templates, no different from `ielts`; user templates (COMMANDS.md, `quiz template`) already let a learner keep their own level-specific ones.

## Left out

- A Japanese interface catalog (`ui_lang: ja`). The learner reads Japanese in the questions; the buttons can stay in English or Vietnamese.
- Embedding a Japanese font.
- Stroke order, handwriting input, furigana generated by the tool. Readings are written by the author or the agent.
- Scoring like the real JLPT (scaled scores per section, a pass mark per section). The usual points apply.

## Cost once the seams exist

Passage groups: the data model, the page layout and the check rules, about a week with tests, shared with IELTS. Furigana, underline and `content-lang`: parser, renderer and rules, a few days. The templates: a day each for content.
